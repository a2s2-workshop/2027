A2S2 2027

AI Auditing of Sociotechnical Systems

A workshop at CHI 2027 for people who study AI and algorithmic systems as they are actually built, deployed, and lived with — including those doing this work without calling it auditing.

About

Auditing has become one of the main ways we hold AI and algorithmic systems to account, but most of the methods and vocabulary we have inherited were built for evaluating models. The systems that actually affect people are larger than that. A recommender is inseparable from the interface that presents it, the incentives of the platform that runs it, the moderation and appeals processes around it, and the organizational decisions that determine what it optimizes for. Auditing the model alone answers a question nobody asked.

This workshop takes the sociotechnical system as the unit of analysis. We are interested in how AI is integrated into products, platforms, organizations, and institutions, and in what auditing has to become in order to say anything useful about that. CHI is the natural venue for this conversation: it is where the interface, the user, the organization, and the model are all treated as part of the same object of study. We deliberately want a blend of academic and practitioner perspectives, and this workshop is explicitly not limited to language models.

We also want to reach people doing this work under other names — AI assurance, independent verification, evaluation, red-teaming, compliance testing, investigative reporting — who may not think of themselves as auditors but are solving the same problems.

Themes & Topics

We invite contributions on, but not limited to, the following themes:

  • Beyond model evaluation Auditing systems as deployed: platforms, pipelines, interfaces, defaults, and the institutions built around them.
  • Data for audits Obtaining, accessing, and generating audit data — data donation, sock puppets and scraping, researcher access regimes, and the legal and infrastructural barriers in the way.
  • Community-centered and participatory audits Who audits, for whom, and with what standing; involving affected communities as more than subjects.
  • The future of audit practice Assurance, independent verification, third-party ecosystems, and the practitioners doing this work without calling it auditing.
  • Accountability and governance Regulation, disclosure, standards, and the harder question of what audits are actually able to change.

Submissions

Participation is by short written contribution. We welcome extended abstracts and position papers of up to four pages (excluding references), which serve as applications to attend. Submissions are non-archival: there are no proceedings, and work presented here remains free to be published elsewhere.

We are less interested in finished results than in arguments and vantage points. What is your view on the future of auditing and evaluation? Is there a shift in vision or strategy you want to put to this community? What does your corner of practice know that the rest of us do not? Contributions describing ongoing work, methods in development, field experience, or failures are all welcome. Again: this workshop is not about language models specifically, and submissions concerning recommender systems, ranking and search, computer vision, risk scoring, and other deployed algorithmic systems are very much in scope.

We are committed to broad participation across disciplines, geographies, seniority levels, and institution types, including participants from outside academia. The workshop will support hybrid participation and we will publish accessibility details alongside the full call. Submission instructions and venue details will be announced here.
